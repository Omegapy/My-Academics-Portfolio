CSC515 Module 5 - Morphology Operations for Handwritten Text Enhancement

========================================================================================
STEP 1/6: Prepare the handwritten note
========================================================================================

The program loads the 996 x 974-pixel scan. It shows a tilted yellow sticky note
attached to a printed form. In extract_note(), four selected corners are used to
straighten the note. A 12-pixel border is then removed. The resulting image is 769 x 772
pixels.

In prepare_binary(), grayscale conversion gives each pixel one brightness value instead
of three color values. Otsu's method selects a threshold of 162 on the 0-255 scale.
Pixels at or below this value become white foreground (255); brighter pixels become
black background (0). The saved and displayed binary images use these same values.

Morphology uses a structuring element, also called a kernel, to select the neighboring
pixels examined around each position. The 3 x 3 elliptical element has five active
positions (1). Positions marked 0 are ignored:
    0 1 0
    1 1 1
    0 1 0

The binary image contains 35,968 white foreground pixels. All four operations use this
same input. No noise is added, and no Gaussian smoothing or opening is performed before
the comparison.

Saved teaching view: figures/01_preparation.png

========================================================================================
STEP 2/6: Dilation: expand the foreground
========================================================================================

Dilation expands the white foreground, making the handwriting thicker. For each
position, it uses the largest value under the five active kernel positions. One white
neighboring pixel can make the output white.

Dilation adds 12,038 foreground pixels, an increase of 33.47%. This can help when a
stroke is weak or disconnected. However, the small white marks in the background also
become larger.

For example, a small gap separates S and e in the word Set in the binary input, but
dilation joins the two letters. This could make character separation more difficult. It
does not mean that a damaged stroke has been repaired.

Saved teaching view: figures/02_dilation.png

========================================================================================
STEP 3/6: Erosion: reduce the foreground
========================================================================================

Erosion removes pixels around the white foreground boundaries, making the strokes
thinner. A pixel remains white only when every active kernel position fits inside the
white handwriting.

Erosion removes 11,806 foreground pixels, or 32.82% of the input foreground. Some small
background specks disappear, but thin portions of letters can also be removed.

In the model label, a thin curved stroke becomes broken near the following i. The
background may look cleaner in some areas, but part of the handwriting has been lost.
See figures/07_stroke_detail.png to compare the stroke before and after erosion.

Saved teaching view: figures/03_erosion.png

========================================================================================
STEP 4/6: Opening: remove small foreground marks
========================================================================================

Opening applies erosion followed by dilation. Erosion removes small foreground
structures, and dilation expands the parts that remain. However, dilation cannot restore
a mark removed completely by erosion.

Opening removes 296 foreground pixels (0.82%) without adding white pixels. Three small
isolated marks near the upper-right background disappear. Most of the handwriting
remains close to its original stroke width.

The curved stroke in the model label stays connected after opening, unlike direct
erosion. Some other small fragments and boundary details are removed. A detached mark
could belong to a letter, so its removal does not automatically mean the writing has
been improved.

Saved teaching view: figures/04_opening.png

========================================================================================
STEP 5/6: Closing: fill small gaps
========================================================================================

Closing applies dilation followed by erosion. Dilation can fill a small hole or gap,
while erosion reduces the expanded outer boundary. A connection made during dilation may
still remain afterward.

Closing adds 282 foreground pixels (0.78%) without removing foreground. The handwriting
changes very little overall, but the small isolated marks in the upper-right background
remain.

The S and e in Set become connected. This reduces the number of connected regions from
87 to 86. The program cannot determine whether this space was a damaged stroke or a
separation between letters. See figures/08_gap_detail.png to compare the gap.

Saved teaching view: figures/05_closing.png

========================================================================================
STEP 6/6: Compare the four operations
========================================================================================

The final comparison shows the straightened color note, its binary input, and the four
morphological results. All four start with the same binary image; they are not a
sequence of processing steps.

    Operation   Foreground     Added   Removed   Change %   Regions
    binary         35,968         0         0       0.00        87
    dilation       48,006    12,038         0      33.47        82
    erosion        24,162         0    11,806     -32.82        83
    opening        35,672         0       296      -0.82        81
    closing        36,250       282         0       0.78        86

The foreground counts show how many white pixels were added or removed. A connected
region can contain several letters or only one small mark. These measurements do not
count characters or indicate whether an OCR system can recognize the handwriting
accurately.

For this note, the 3 x 3 opening removes several background marks without the thicker
strokes of dilation or the broken curve seen with erosion. Closing changes fewer
foreground pixels, but it joins the space between S and e in Set. Much of the
improvement in paper contrast comes from Otsu thresholding before morphology. These
results do not mean opening will be best for every handwritten note.

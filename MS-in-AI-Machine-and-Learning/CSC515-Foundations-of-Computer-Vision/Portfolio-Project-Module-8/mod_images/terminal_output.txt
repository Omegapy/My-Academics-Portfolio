========================================================================================
STEP 1/7: Examine the three photographs
========================================================================================

This assignment uses three color photographs from the web. The portrait shows one
primarily front-facing person under indoor lighting. The outdoor photograph shows a
group from head to feet. Its faces occupy a much smaller part of the scene, so that
photograph also supplies the multiple-person and distant-face cases. The third
photograph contains a cat, which supplies the nonhuman example.

Look at the complete photographs before looking at the detections. No original has been
cropped or recolored. The program checks each file's SHA-256, a fingerprint of its
bytes, so the reference coordinates cannot silently be applied to a different version of
the photograph. The source credits are kept with the results.

Human portrait: 2615 x 3269 pixels. Grayscale mean 111.63; standard deviation 72.60.

Full-body group: 3000 x 1996 pixels. Grayscale mean 164.04; standard deviation 84.54.

Cat: nonhuman example: 451 x 300 pixels. Grayscale mean 119.48; standard deviation
32.12.

OpenCV reads the color channels in BGR order: blue, green, and red. cvtColor() converts
them into one uint8 intensity value per pixel, from 0 to 255. The first face experiment
uses this original-resolution grayscale image without smoothing or contrast adjustment.
Matplotlib converts display copies to RGB. Reducing a teaching view does not change the
detector input.

The images have visibly different illumination and colors, but they also show different
subjects and backgrounds. Their brightness statistics describe the whole scenes. Those
numbers do not isolate the effect of lighting alone, and the actual distance between the
camera and each person is not known.

This run uses Python 3.12.13; OpenCV 4.13.0; NumPy 2.5.3; Matplotlib 3.11.2. Platform:
darwin. Processing uses one OpenCV worker thread.

========================================================================================
STEP 2/7: Detect faces with the initial settings
========================================================================================

The initial classifier is haarcascade_frontalface_default.xml. This is a pretrained Haar
cascade, not a person-identification model. It examines intensity patterns at different
positions and sizes. A returned box is a face candidate, not proof that the enclosed
region is a human face.

The baseline uses scaleFactor=1.1, minNeighbors=5, and minSize=(24, 24). scaleFactor
sets the spacing between search scales. minNeighbors sets the support required when
nearby candidates are grouped. minSize excludes smaller search windows. None of these
settings is a calibrated probability of correctness.

Look at the red boxes and compare them with the original scenes. All returned candidates
remain visible, including background mistakes and duplicate boxes. The separate
reference view shows approximate visual annotations made from the original photographs.
Those marks never enter the detector or choose an eye pair.

A detected face is matched to at most one reference face. Intersection-over-union
divides the overlapping area by the combined area of the boxes. Here, a match requires
at least 0.50. The matching routine first maximizes the number of valid one-to-one
matches, then their total overlap. An extra box is a false positive, and an unmatched
reference face is a false negative.

Across the three baseline images, this run produced 7 matched faces, 9 extra boxes, and
0 missed reference faces. Read the tables in the next step to see where they occurred.

========================================================================================
STEP 3/7: Compare the revisions
========================================================================================

Each revision starts from the stored original grayscale image. The second experiment
changes only the face model to haarcascade_frontalface_alt.xml. The third retains that
model but caps the longer scene edge at 1,000 pixels, preserving its proportions. This
changes the sampling grid and the effective original-image size represented by the
24-pixel minimum search window.

The fourth experiment applies global histogram equalization to the scaled input. The
fifth instead uses CLAHE with clipLimit=2.0 and an 8 x 8 tile grid. Both are compared
with the plain scaled image, not chained together. The sixth returns to plain grayscale
and increases minNeighbors from 5 to 7. A stricter setting can remove a false positive,
but it can also remove a real face.

========================================================================================
STEP 4/7: Extract faces and estimate eye positions
========================================================================================

The red boxes were drawn on display copies. Face extraction uses the untouched original
color photographs instead. The selected detector boxes are mapped back with the actual
horizontal and vertical resize factors, then used to slice the original arrays. NumPy
uses [y, x], while each OpenCV rectangle is (x, y, width, height).

A separate face copy is resized to a maximum edge of 300 pixels for the provisional eye
search. Enlarging a small face changes how the cascade samples it; it does not restore
detail that the camera did not record. The clean original crop is kept for the later
geometric transformation.

The first eye search uses haarcascade_eye.xml on the upper 70% of the crop. Overlapping
proposals are filtered, and candidate pairs are checked for plausible spacing, height,
size, and placement on opposite sides of the face. These checks can reject a nose or
brow proposal, but they do not establish that a pair is correct.

When no pair passes, the program tries equalization and the eye-tree-eyeglasses cascade.
The last attempts search the upper left and right halves separately with a finer scale
step and lower grouping requirement. That last revision changes more than one setting,
so its effect cannot be attributed to the region alone. Every attempted candidate list
is saved. A missing pair remains unresolved.

========================================================================================
STEP 5/7: Align faces and find the eyes again
========================================================================================

A first eye estimate is needed before the face can be oriented. align_face() calculates
the angle between those centers with atan2(), then uses their distance to calculate one
uniform scale. The translation moves their midpoint to the center of the target eye
line. This performs rotation, scaling, and centering; it is more than a square resize.

The common canvas is 320 x 320 pixels. Its target eye centers are (96, 121.6) and (224,
121.6), which puts them at 30% and 70% of the width and 38% of the height. warpAffine()
samples the original crop using the calculated matrix. Reflected pixels can fill the
display border, but a separate validity mask prevents those artificial border pixels
from becoming treatment data.

Now look at the eye boxes on the aligned faces. They come from a new cascade search
after the transformation. They are not the original boxes moved into place. The
provisional estimates, final detections, transformation matrix, inverse matrix, and all
retry settings are retained separately.

The two estimated control points map to their targets within a small numerical
tolerance. That checks the transformation mathematics, not the true eye locations. The
program also transforms the independent reference eye marks and measures their remaining
tilt. Approximate cascade-window centers can leave real eyes slightly misaligned even
when the control-point check passes.

========================================================================================
STEP 6/7: Blur the eyes and restore the scenes
========================================================================================

The eye treatment starts from each aligned color crop. The first margin is 35% of the
detected eye-box width and height. The final margin increases to 60% on every face to
cover more of the eye boundaries. Gaussian blur replaces pixels only within those padded
regions. The comparison also shows a weaker sigma of 10% of the eye-box size beside a
stronger final sigma of 45%. The minimum sigma is 2 pixels. The actual odd kernel and
sigma are saved for each eye. Both padding and strength change, so this comparison does
not isolate blur strength.

Look for remaining eye detail in the weaker version before examining the final one. The
final blur is deliberately strong, which also removes some surrounding detail inside the
padded regions. A less visible seam would not necessarily mean better privacy. These
pictures show the treatment; they are not a re-identification test.

The inverse transformation maps the blurred pixels and their binary mask back to the
original crop. Color uses linear interpolation, while the mask uses nearest-neighbor
interpolation. Only pixels selected by that mask are replaced in the full scene. The
program checks exact equality outside the mask rather than trusting that the two images
look similar.

Human portrait: 1 face(s) received eye treatment; 125,130 pixels changed; outside-mask
pixels unchanged: True.

Full-body group: 6 face(s) received eye treatment; 23,920 pixels changed; outside-mask
pixels unchanged: True.

Cat: nonhuman example: 0 face(s) received eye treatment; 0 pixels changed; outside-mask
pixels unchanged: True.

There is no animal-specific branch that forces the cat to remain unchanged. It enters
the same human-face pipeline. When the selected detector returns no face candidates, the
treatment mask is empty and the original decoded pixels remain unchanged. Any false face
detection would still be reported.

========================================================================================
STEP 7/7: Review the results and their limits
========================================================================================

The selected face experiment matched 7 of 7 reference faces, with 0 extra boxes and 0
misses. Eye processing produced 14 final eye centers on 7 face candidates. Unresolved
face candidates: 0.

Under the fixed eye-center rule, 6 of 14 reference eyes were localized within tolerance.
Both centers met that rule on 2 faces. The tolerance is 10% of the reference eye
separation, with a two-pixel minimum, measured in the original image. A cascade window
is an eye region, not an exact pupil landmark. Narrow or partly closed eyes make that
distinction important.

The initial mask fully covered 12 reference eye regions. The final treatment mask fully
covered 14 of 14 marked eye regions. Coverage and center accuracy answer different
questions. A padded blur can cover an eye even when the center of its detection window
falls outside the localization tolerance. Neither count alone proves that the person
cannot be identified.

The reference marks are approximate visual annotations, not an independently audited
benchmark. Inspect the saved reference overlays, especially the small eyes in the group
image. The CSV reports each eye's error, tolerance, coverage, and remaining alignment
angle rather than hiding the weaker measurements inside an overall success label.

All three photographs were used during development. The program demonstrates what these
settings do on this small set, not how accurately they will process a new population of
faces. Eye blurring also leaves other identifying details, including the rest of the
face, clothing, and name patches. The saved outputs are an assignment demonstration, not
a guarantee of anonymization.

All images, tables, detector attempts, reference marks, and this explanation were saved
before the first interactive view. The APA summary is a separate assignment deliverable
and is not generated by this script.
